\section{Unmanned Aerial Vehicles}%

\subsection{Definition of a UAV}%
\label{subsec:bg:uav-definition}

An \glsxtrfull{abb:uav}, commonly called a drone, is an aircraft that operates
without a pilot on board. It can be controlled remotely by a human operator or
autonomously by software running on the vehicle. A \gls{abb:uav} is also equipped
with sensors selected according to its mission~\cite{kumar2025comprehensive}.

\Glspl{abb:uav} are used in many fields, including surveillance, monitoring,
search and rescue, and mapping~\cite{kumar2025comprehensive}. Multirotor
\glspl{abb:uav} are particularly useful for repetitive or risky activities, such
as warehouse inventory management and infrastructure inspection, because they
reduce the need for human involvement~\cite{tejeroruiz2024realistic}.

\subsection{Main Categories of UAVs}%
\label{subsec:bg:uav-categories}

The literature generally distinguishes four main types of \glspl{abb:uav}:
fixed-wing, single-rotor, multirotor, and hybrid
platforms~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.

\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.8em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ Fixed-wing UAVs:] These \glspl{abb:uav} use wings to generate lift and
          are designed for efficient and fast flight. They offer long endurance
          and are therefore suitable for large-area surveys and structure
          inspection~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}. However, they cannot hover and
          require a conventional take-off and landing
          procedure~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}. These constraints make them
          unsuitable for confined indoor environments.

    \item[\textbullet\ Single-rotor UAVs:] Similar to helicopters, single-rotor
          \glspl{abb:uav} can hover and carry relatively large
          payloads~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}. However, their mechanical
          structure is more complex, and they are generally more expensive and
          difficult to operate~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.

    \item[\textbullet\ Multirotor UAVs:] Multirotor platforms are widely used for imaging and
          inspection because of their low cost, compact size, manoeuvrability,
          and ability to take off, land, and hover
          vertically~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}. Quadrotors are the most common
          configuration. Their main limitations are shorter flight time and
          lower payload capacity compared with larger
          platforms~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.

    \item[\textbullet\ Hybrid UAVs:] Hybrid platforms combine characteristics of fixed-wing
          and rotary-wing \glspl{abb:uav}. They can perform vertical take-off
          and landing and can hover, while also providing better flight
          efficiency than conventional multirotors~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
          However, their design and control are more
          complex~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{\textbullet\ Suitability for indoor applications:}
Among these types, multirotor \glspl{abb:uav} are particularly suitable for
indoor inspection. Their ability to hover and move freely in three dimensions
allows them to approach objects closely~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}, and their
compact size lets them operate in narrow aisles and at different rack
levels~\cite{pore2026uavqr}. Vertical take-off and landing platforms have
therefore become widely used in warehousing
operations~\cite{kalinov2020warevision}, and recent inspection systems rely on
quadrotors in both simulation and real experiments~\cite{pore2026uavqr}. However,
autonomous control remains challenging because the drone must plan and execute
its movements safely in a confined and complex
environment~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.

\subsection{Components of a UAV Platform}%
\label{subsec:bg:uav-components}

A \gls{abb:uav} platform combines several subsystems that work together to
provide flight, sensing, computation, and communication.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Airframe and propulsion system}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.4em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ Frame:] provides the mechanical structure of the
          \gls{abb:uav} and supports its main components~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Motors:] convert electrical energy into mechanical
          rotation to drive the propellers~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Propellers:] generate the thrust required for flight and
          movement. Their number and arrangement define the multirotor
          configuration, with quadrotors being the most
          common~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Flight control system}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.4em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ Flight controller:] processes sensor measurements and
          generates the commands required to maintain the \gls{abb:uav}'s
          stability and control its
          movement~\cite{pore2026drone, mohsan2023unmanned}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Autopilot:] provides higher-level functions such as
          mission planning and waypoint generation~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
          PX4 and ArduPilot are widely used open-source autopilot
          stacks~\cite{acharya2020cornet}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Low-level control:] regulates the \gls{abb:uav}'s
          altitude, velocity, and orientation so that it follows the requested
          trajectory. \Gls{abb:pid} controllers are widely used because they are
          simple to implement~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Onboard sensors}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.4em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ IMU:] combines an accelerometer and a gyroscope to measure
          linear acceleration and angular motion~\cite{jacinto2024pegasus}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Barometer and magnetometer:] provide altitude and heading
          information, respectively~\cite{chandran2024network, jacinto2024pegasus}.
    \item[\textbullet\ RGB camera:] provides visual information and is commonly
          used for barcode and \gls{abb:qr} code detection in warehouse
          applications~\cite{pore2026drone}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Depth camera and LiDAR:] provide distance information for
          mapping and obstacle detection~\cite{pore2026drone, chang2023review}.
    \item[\textbullet\ ToF sensors:] measure distance from the travel time of a
          signal, with accuracy decreasing as the distance
          grows~\cite{tejeroruiz2024realistic}. They are therefore suited to
          short-range proximity detection~\cite{pore2026drone}.
    \item[\textbullet\ GNSS/GPS:] provides global positioning for outdoor
          flight~\cite{jacinto2024pegasus}. Since \gls{abb:gps} is unreliable
          indoors, other positioning methods are required in warehouse
          environments~\cite{pore2026uavqr}.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Onboard computing and communication}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.4em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ Companion computer:] runs the high-level logic of the
          mission, separately from the flight control
          unit~\cite{acharya2020cornet}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Communication interfaces:] allow the \gls{abb:uav} to
          exchange information with a ground station and, in
          multi-\gls{abb:uav} systems, with other
          drones~\cite{chandran2024network, acharya2020cornet}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Wi-Fi and telemetry radio:] Wi-Fi links are commonly used
          for data exchange in warehouse prototypes~\cite{cristiani2020inventory},
          while the flight control unit uses a radio modem for telemetry and
          flight control~\cite{acharya2020cornet}.
\end{description}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Power supply}
\begin{description}[style=unboxed, leftmargin=0pt, itemsep=0.4em,
        font=\normalfont\bfseries]
    \item[\textbullet\ Battery:] supplies energy to all \gls{abb:uav} subsystems
          and directly affects flight
          endurance~\cite{kumar2025comprehensive, mohsan2023unmanned}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Flight endurance:] depends mainly on vehicle weight, size,
          and payload~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}. Small \glspl{abb:uav} typically
          fly for twenty to thirty minutes, so limited battery capacity restricts
          the duration of an inspection mission~\cite{mohsan2023unmanned}.
    \item[\textbullet\ Extended operation:] multiple \glspl{abb:uav} can
          distribute the workload~\cite{kumar2025comprehensive}, while some
          systems use ground charging stations that the drone visits when its
          battery falls below a threshold~\cite{cristiani2020inventory}.
\end{description}
