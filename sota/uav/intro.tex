\section{Chapter Introduction}%
\label{sec:bg:intro}

Warehouses are an important part of modern supply chains, and accurate inventory
tracking is essential for their operation. Traditionally, inventory was managed
manually using paper records and later barcode scanners. Although barcode
scanners improved the process, inventory inspection still requires significant
manual work and remains prone to errors~\cite{pore2026drone, pore2026uavqr}.

The rapid growth of e-commerce has increased the need for faster and more
accurate inventory information~\cite{kalinov2020warevision}. \Glspl{abb:uav} have
therefore been proposed as an alternative for warehouse inspection. Their small
size and mobility allow them to move through narrow aisles and reach different
levels of storage racks while reducing manual effort~\cite{pore2026uavqr}. They
also remove some of the repetitive and dangerous tasks previously performed by
warehouse operators~\cite{kalinov2020warevision}. \Glspl{abb:qr} codes are often
used to identify items during these inspections.

However, using \glspl{abb:uav} for inventory inspection introduces new
challenges. Indoor warehouses generally do not provide reliable \gls{abb:gps}
signals, so the drone must estimate its position using onboard
sensors~\cite{pore2026uavqr}. \Gls{abb:qr} code reading is also more difficult
during flight because motion blur, lighting conditions, and occlusion can reduce
recognition accuracy~\cite{dong2024motionblurred, alsulami2026deep}. In addition,
the gap between simulation and real-world conditions can affect the performance
of vision-based systems~\cite{pore2026uavqr}.

Using multiple \glspl{abb:uav} can further improve mission efficiency by allowing
different areas to be inspected in parallel. However, this requires coordination
and information sharing between the drones to avoid redundant
inspections~\cite{pore2026uavqr}.

This chapter presents the main concepts and technologies related to this work.
\Cref{sec:bg:uav} introduces \glspl{abb:uav}, including their main components,
indoor flight, multi-\gls{abb:uav} systems, and simulation environments.
\Cref{sec:bg:inspection} presents warehouse inventory inspection and existing
automated approaches. \Cref{sec:bg:qr} focuses on \gls{abb:qr} codes, their
detection, and the challenges of reading them from \glspl{abb:uav}. Finally,
\Cref{sec:bg:conclusion} summarises the main points of the chapter.
