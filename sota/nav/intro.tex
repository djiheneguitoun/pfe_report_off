\section{Chapter Introduction}%
\label{sec:nav:intro}

Inspecting warehouse inventory with a \gls{abb:uav} is fundamentally a problem of
deciding what to do next. At every moment the drone has to choose where to look,
which rack to inspect, and how to react to events that were not anticipated. These
decisions form a closed loop between perception, reasoning, and action, and they
have to be made under tight constraints: a \gls{abb:gps}-denied space, partial and
noisy observations, dynamic obstacles, and a limited onboard compute and energy
budget.

The literature offers several families of methods for this kind of problem, from
classical path planning to \gls{abb:rl}, Active Inference, and the recent
integration of large language and vision-language models. They differ in their
assumptions, their reasoning style, and their suitability to operate in a partially
known environment. This chapter organizes these families, compares them along a
common set of criteria, and positions the inventory inspection task within them.

\Cref{sec:nav:planning} reviews path planning and coverage, including the
learning-based methods that build on \gls{abb:mdp} and \gls{abb:pomdp} formulations
and \gls{abb:rl}. \Cref{sec:nav:activeinf} develops Active Inference for
\gls{abb:uav} systems.
\Cref{sec:nav:genai} examines generative AI and \gls{abb:llm} integration.
\Cref{sec:nav:synthesis} proposes a cross-cutting synthesis. \Cref{sec:nav:gaps}
discusses the remaining gaps and positions the work realized so far in this thesis.
\Cref{sec:nav:conclusion} concludes.
