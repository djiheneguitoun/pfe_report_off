\section{Chapter Introduction}%
\label{sec:nav:intro}

The previous chapter showed that the platform and the perception pipeline are no
longer the limiting factor in warehouse inspection. The bottleneck lies one level
higher, in the way the drone decides where to go next. Most existing systems
execute a route that was computed before take-off, over a layout assumed to be
known, and under the hypothesis that the environment will remain unchanged for
the duration of the mission.

This chapter examines that decision layer. It compares the main families of
autonomous navigation methods around a single criterion: the moment at which the
path is committed. Some approaches resolve the entire route before the vehicle
leaves the ground; others commit only to the next move, and derive it from what
the drone has just observed. This distinction defines the two groups studied
here.

\Cref{sec:nav:principles} states the two principles and identifies the cases
where the boundary between them becomes ambiguous.
\Cref{sec:nav:classical,sec:nav:metaheuristics,sec:nav:openloop-limits} cover the
open-loop methods, first the classical algorithms and then the biologically
inspired ones, and close on the limits they share.
\Cref{sec:nav:learning,sec:nav:activeinf,sec:nav:genai} cover the closed-loop
methods, from learning-based approaches to active inference and to the recent use
of language and vision-language models. \Cref{sec:nav:conclusion} summarises the
chapter.
